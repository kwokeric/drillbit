Five screens

1 · Goal

What the app is for

Short sessions of 5 to 10 minutes, in a line or on the train. Every card tests recognition and reasoning, not typing. Any card can be answered with taps, or at most one short line of code.

Three rules shape everything below: answers live in the bottom third of the screen where your thumb is, the app works with no signal, and closing it mid-session loses nothing.

2 · Content

Four card types

Spot the bug

Find the broken line

A 5–10 line function and a symptom ("callers get []"). Tap the line.

Answer: tap a line → reason + one-line fix → grade

JS / TS fundamentals

Know the language

Event loop, closures, this, coercion, array methods, promises, TS narrowing and generics.

Answer: multiple choice, predict output, or flip card

Algo patterns

Name the approach

A LeetCode problem summarized in 1–2 sentences, with a link. You choose the patterns, and the card reveals the key insight, a 3-step outline, complexity and the usual gotcha.

Answer: pick pattern chips → reveal → grade

One-liners

Muscle memory

Idioms you'll type in every interview: dedupe, numeric sort, grid init, frequency map.

Answer: type one line → auto-check or self-grade

3 · Sessions

How studying works

Each card has a schedule. After answering, you tap one of two buttons, Repeat (retry arrow) or Okay (thumbs up), and the app picks when to show it next. This uses a simplified SM-2 algorithm, the same family Anki uses. You don't need to understand the math. Cards you miss come back soon, and cards you know drift out to weeks.

  • Daily: everything due today plus 5 new cards. Around 10 minutes.
  • Deck drill: one card type, e.g. only algo patterns.
  • Weak spots: the cards and patterns you miss most.
  • Cram: pick tags (e.g. graphs, promises) and ignore the schedule. For the night before an interview.

Repeat puts the card back into this session a few cards later, and it comes back tomorrow. Okay means you're done with it, and the next gap grows (1 day, 3 days, then longer). You can also swipe: right for Okay, left for Repeat. An OKAY or REPEAT stamp fades in as you drag, like a dating-app card.

Hints. Every card has a Hint button next to the answer controls. One tap shows a nudge that points at the idea without naming the answer. The session summary counts hints so Weak spots can find them. Cards with no written hint get an automatic one: bug cards narrow to a 3-line region, multiple choice removes one wrong option, and pattern cards strike out half the wrong chips.

4 · Using LeetCode

Problems as references, patterns as the lesson

Each pattern card stores the LeetCode number, slug, difficulty and link. The prompt is a 1–2 sentence summary in our own words, and the full statement is one tap away on leetcode.com. Problem statements are LeetCode's content, so we summarize and link instead of copying them.

The seed list follows a well-known curated set (Blind 75 / NeetCode 150) grouped by pattern, so every pattern has 3–6 problems. Some cards have more than one right answer, for example "sort first" plus "two pointers". Picking a subset counts as partial credit and the card shows what you missed.

Where the cards come from

Nothing is generated when the app opens. Every card is written ahead of time as a JSON file in content/. At build time, Vite bundles those files into the app, the zod check rejects any malformed card, and the service worker caches the result. Opening the app on the train reads cards from that cache, with no network request.

Cards are written by hand, or drafted by Claude in a coding session and then reviewed and edited by you before they're committed. Writing them ahead of time matters for this app: a bug card is only useful if exactly one line is wrong, and an MCQ only if the distractors are plausible but wrong. Those need checking, and a card generated on the fly can't be checked.

The only thing that changes on the phone is your progress (due dates, ease, hints used), which lives in IndexedDB.

Adding a card means committing a JSON file. GitHub's mobile web editor works, so you can write cards from your phone too, and each push redeploys.

7 · Plan

Roadmap

v1 · built

Usable MVP. The four card types plus flip cards, hints, Daily session, Deck drill, Weak spots, Cram by tag, scheduler, session summary, basic stats, export/import, 68 seed cards, PWA install. Next step: deploy on Vercel.

v1.1

Study smarter. Richer stats by pattern and tag, bookmarks, deck grows to ~150 cards.

v2

Card drafting with Claude Code. No generate button in the app. Ask Claude Code, on your computer or at claude.ai/code from your phone, to draft cards ("6 sliding-window pattern cards with hints"). It writes the JSON, runs the checks and opens a pull request. You review and merge, and the site redeploys. This uses your Claude subscription, so no API key and no backend. Optionally, a scheduled weekly task drafts cards for your weak patterns. Cross-device sync only if needed.
